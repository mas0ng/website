What the ICO actually does

What the Information Commissioner's Office does, what it does not do, and when it makes sense to complain to it.

The ICO is the UK's independent regulator for data protection and related information rights. It is the Information Commissioner's Office. It is not a general consumer-help desk, and it is not a court.

What it is for

The ICO oversees how organisations handle personal data under UK data protection law. It also has a role in the cookie and electronic-communications rules, and in some other information-rights regimes.

In practice that means it:

  • publishes guidance organisations are expected to take seriously
  • can investigate complaints about data handling
  • can require information, issue notices, and in serious cases impose fines
  • explains your rights in public language, including access, deletion, and objection

It is the body named in most UK privacy policies when they tell you how to complain.

What it does not do

The ICO will not rewrite a contract, recover money like a small-claims court, or police every annoying banner on the internet in real time. It does not exist to take sides in a customer-service dispute that is not actually about personal data.

It also does not decide, in advance, that a particular website is “approved”. An organisation can be following the law and still have a clunky policy. It can have a beautiful policy and still handle data badly.

A complaint is not an emergency 999 call. If you think data is at immediate risk, tell the organisation as well. If a crime is involved, that is a police matter too.

When a complaint makes sense

Complain to the organisation first if you can. Many problems are a missed email, a wrong address, or a subject access request that needs a nudge.

The ICO becomes useful when:

  • the organisation ignores a rights request
  • it refuses without a reason that makes sense
  • it appears to be using personal data in a way the policy never described
  • cookie or marketing practices look clearly non-compliant, not merely ugly

You do not have to wait forever. You also do not have to use a particular script. Stick to facts: who, what data, what you asked for, what happened, and what you want.

Fines are not the usual outcome

News stories about large fines are real and rare. Most ICO work is guidance, correspondence, and getting an organisation to fix a process. That is still the point. A regulator that only existed to collect headlines would be a worse regulator.

If you want a copy of your data, ask the organisation. If you want the law explained, the ICO's own guidance is the public reference. If you want compensation, that is a different legal route.

This article is an explainer, not a complaint, and not legal advice.
