How AI uses your prompts

What happens to a prompt, files, and replies when you use an AI feature, and why generated output still needs checking.

This is an explainer, not a legal policy. The rules that apply on mas0ng.com are in the AI Policy and the Privacy Policy. If this article and those documents ever disagree, the policies are the ones that count.

What a prompt is

A prompt is the text you send to an AI feature: a question, an instruction, a draft, or a follow-up. The useful part of the system is not magic. It is a model that predicts a reply from the text and other context it is given.

That context can include earlier messages in the same conversation, settings you chose, and text taken from a file you attached. If you would not put a sentence in an email to a service provider, think twice before putting it in a prompt.

Where the prompt goes

When an authorised user uses an AI feature on mas0ng.com, the prompt and relevant context may be sent to the external AI or model provider selected for that request. The provider's own terms and privacy information also apply to its processing.

That is the important mechanical fact. A private login on this site does not mean the model runs only on this site. The reply has to be generated somewhere, and for these features that somewhere is an external provider.

Providers may process information outside the UK. Where mas0ng.com is responsible for a restricted international transfer, that route is assessed against UK data protection law before it is used. The AI Policy is the place for that detail.

Files are not just filenames

Readable text files may be included as context. Images and PDFs may be sent to an external provider to extract visible text, even when a different model is then used for the conversation. The extracted text can be included in the later request.

A file can still be stored and shown as attached even if nothing useful could be extracted from it. Uploading a document is not the same as “the model glanced at the title”. Assume the contents may be processed.

Do not upload passwords, private keys, authentication secrets, unnecessary personal data, or someone else's confidential material.

Saved chats are storage, not a vault

Chats and files may be saved so you can reopen them. Deleting a chat removes the saved conversation and attached files from mas0ng.com, subject to temporary infrastructure or backup copies until they rotate.

Retention depends on whether something is saved, temporary, or no longer recent. Older attachments may be removed while conversation text remains. The external provider may also keep its own service records under the settings and terms that apply to that request.

A deleted chat on this site is not a guarantee that no copy exists anywhere. It is the local deletion of the saved feature data, plus the residual copies any hosted service has while backups rotate.

What the model does not know

AI output can be fluent and still be wrong. It can be incomplete, biased, outdated, or confidently invented. It is not a substitute for legal, medical, financial, security, or other professional advice.

mas0ng.com does not use AI output to make solely automated decisions about public visitors or authorised users that produce legal or similarly significant effects. You are still responsible for checking anything you publish or act on.

The current edu0 textbook reader does not send account details, passwords, reading progress, or textbook pages to an AI provider. Linking the AI Policy from edu0 does not mean AI processing is happening while you read.

What not to put in a prompt

  • Passwords, API keys, authentication codes, private keys, or other secrets
  • Special-category, criminal-offence, financial, identity, or health information unless the feature expressly supports it and there is a lawful and necessary reason
  • Another person's personal data or confidential material without a lawful basis and any permission that is required
  • Requests for unlawful, abusive, exploitative, or security-bypassing output

Access control is not confidentiality. A signed-in feature can still send text to an external provider.
