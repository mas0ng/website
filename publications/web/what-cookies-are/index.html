What cookies are

A plain-language explanation of browser cookies, similar storage, and why websites keep small pieces of data on a device.

What a cookie actually is

A cookie is a small piece of text that a website asks your browser to store. On a later visit, the browser can send that text back with the request. That is how a site can recognise a returning browser without you typing anything.

The word is unhelpful. A cookie is not a program, it does not scan your files, and it does not by itself identify you the way a name or email address does. It is a labelled value, more like a ticket stub than a listening device.

A typical cookie has a name, a value, an expiry, and rules about which site and path it belongs to. Extra flags can limit when it is sent:

  • Secure means it should only travel over HTTPS.
  • HttpOnly means page scripts cannot read it.
  • SameSite limits whether it is sent when you arrive from another website.

Those flags matter, but they are still just rules for one small stored value.

Cookies are not the only kind of browser storage

Websites can also use local storage and session storage. Those sit in the browser too, but they are not attached to every later request the way cookies are. A page has to read them with its own script.

Session storage usually lasts until that browser tab is closed. Local storage stays until the site replaces it or you clear it. mas0ng.com uses local storage for some interface choices, including the preference that stops an informational privacy notice from appearing again immediately after you dismiss it.

If you only block cookies, you may still have local or session storage. If you clear “cookies and site data”, you often clear all three.

Why a site uses them

The honest uses are usually mundane:

  • keeping you signed in
  • remembering a setting you chose
  • stopping a notice from repeating
  • protecting a form or sign-in against automated abuse

The less honest uses are tracking: remembering you across sites so that advertising networks can build a picture of what you look at. That is what most people mean when they say they “hate cookies”. The technology is the same. The purpose is not.

Public pages on mas0ng.com do not currently use cookies or similar storage for behavioural advertising, cross-site tracking, or third-party advertising analytics.

First-party and third-party cookies

A first-party cookie belongs to the site in the address bar. A third-party cookie belongs to a different site that has been embedded, for example an advert, a social widget, or a tracker.

Third-party cookies are the ones most often used to follow people from site to site. Many browsers now block or restrict them by default. That is useful, but it does not make every first-party cookie harmless, and it does not mean a site has stopped sharing data in other ways.

Consent, necessity, and UK rules

In the UK, storing or accessing information on your device is a separate issue from “is this personal data?”. The cookie rules generally require clear information and consent unless a legal exception applies.

Authentication, security, fraud prevention, and storage that is strictly necessary to provide something you have asked for can qualify for an exception when the legal conditions are met. A sign-in cookie that exists only to keep an authorised session working is a common example.

Optional analytics, extra appearance features, or advertising storage usually need a genuine choice. mas0ng.com does not currently use a consent banner for public-page analytics, because it does not currently run that kind of public-page analytics.

“We use cookies to improve your experience” is not an explanation. A site should say what is stored, why, and whether you can refuse it.

How this site uses them

The current picture is small:

  • Public pages may remember that you dismissed an informational notice. That preference is a local interface choice, not an advertising profile.
  • Protected sign-in features use essential cookies and related browser storage to authenticate users, keep sessions, and reduce abuse. Blocking those will usually sign you out or prevent sign-in.
  • Cloudflare Turnstile is loaded only when a feature needs a security challenge, such as sign-in or the private security-report form. It may store or access device and request information to tell people from bots.
  • Some tools keep local state you asked for, such as interface preferences or draft data. That state stays on the device unless the feature says it will be uploaded.
  • The edu0 textbook reader at edu.mas0ng.com sets one application sign-in cookie after a successful login. It is necessary to provide the private library. Sign-out or expiry ends the session.

The Cookie Notice is the inventory of current storage. This article is the background; that page is the policy.

How you can control them

Every mainstream browser lets you:

  • see which sites have stored cookies
  • delete them
  • block third-party cookies
  • block all cookies

Blocking storage that a feature needs will break that feature. Public pages on this site should remain readable. Protected areas will not.

Clearing site data is a local action. It does not delete an account, server-side reading progress, or files stored in a private service.
