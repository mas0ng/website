How to identify the applicable licence

Each repository, package, download, asset, or release should identify its terms in a LICENSE, COPYING, notice, download page, repository metadata, or other clear licence statement. The exact licence text supplied with the item controls.

The summaries below are only a practical overview. They do not replace the licence text, create extra permissions, or change third-party rights. If no licence is specified, copyright law applies and no additional permission is granted.

MIT licence

The MIT licence is a permissive open-source licence. In general, it allows use, copying, modification, distribution, sublicensing, and commercial use of the covered software, subject to the conditions in the licence.

  • Copies or substantial portions must retain the copyright notice and licence text.
  • The software is supplied under the warranty and liability disclaimer contained in the MIT licence.
  • The licence does not grant rights in material that is not actually covered by it.

GNU GPL version 3

GNU GPLv3 is a strong copyleft open-source licence. It permits running, studying, modifying, and conveying covered software, but conveying covered works can trigger source-code, notice, and licence obligations.

  • When you convey a covered work, you must comply with the GPL's corresponding-source and notice requirements that apply to that work.
  • Modified covered files and works may need to carry notices and remain under GPLv3 when conveyed.
  • Whether a particular combination is a derivative work or a separate aggregate is fact-specific; the exact GPL text controls.
  • The GPL contains its own warranty and liability provisions.

Apache License 2.0

Apache License 2.0 is a permissive open-source licence that includes an express patent licence from contributors, subject to its terms.

  • You may generally use, modify, reproduce, distribute, and sublicense covered material, including commercially.
  • You must provide a copy of the licence, preserve required notices, mark modified files as changed, and handle any NOTICE file as the licence requires.
  • The licence does not itself grant permission to use contributor names, trademarks, service marks, or product names except as allowed by the licence or applicable law.
  • The patent licence and termination provisions in the exact Apache 2.0 text apply.

Creative Commons BY-NC-ND

CC BY-NC-ND may be used for particular assets, media, or documentation only where the material expressly says that licence applies. It permits sharing the licensed material subject to attribution, non-commercial use, and no-distribution-of-adaptations conditions in the exact licence.

  • Give the attribution and licence information required by the applicable CC licence.
  • Do not use the material for a commercial purpose unless separate permission is granted.
  • Do not distribute adapted material under an ND licence, although rights to make private changes may differ from rights to share them.

Proprietary and closed-source material

Private applications, hosted services, APIs, backend code, premium assets, and some downloads may be proprietary. Access to a hosted feature does not grant a licence to copy or redistribute the underlying implementation.

  • Use the service only through authorised interfaces and within account or feature limits.
  • Do not copy, redistribute, rent, lease, sublicense, or commercially exploit proprietary material without permission.
  • Do not bypass licensing or access controls.
  • Any restriction on reverse engineering applies only to the extent permitted by law and does not remove mandatory statutory rights.

Default: all rights reserved

If a repository, asset, file, or page does not state a licence, the default position is that copyright and other applicable rights are reserved. Public availability does not by itself authorise copying, modification, republication, redistribution, or commercial reuse.

You may still exercise any right that the law gives you independently of permission, such as a statutory copyright exception where its conditions are met.

Third-party material and trademarks

A mas0ng.com project may contain third-party code, fonts, artwork, APIs, names, or other material with separate terms. The licence for mas0ng.com's original material does not override those third-party terms.

Trade marks, logos, and brand names are not licensed merely because software code is open source. Any trade-mark use must be separately permitted by the owner or by applicable law.

Commercial permissions and questions

If you want permission beyond the licence that applies to a project or asset, request it before using the material. A separate written permission may impose additional conditions and applies only to the material and use it identifies.
