Who is responsible and UK scope

Mason, the UK-based operator of mas0ng.com, is the controller responsible for personal data handled through this service. Questions, rights requests, and data protection complaints can be sent to mason@mas0ng.com.

The service is operated from the United Kingdom and is directed to people in the UK. It is not marketed to people outside the UK. Access from another country may still be technically possible, and describing the intended UK audience does not remove any law that applies regardless of that intention.

Important: If mas0ng.com is operated commercially or under a legal trading identity, the public legal information should identify the correct legal person and any address or registration details that UK law requires for that activity.

Purposes and lawful bases

Where UK data protection law applies, personal data is processed only where a lawful basis applies. The principal bases are:

  • Legitimate interests: delivering and maintaining the site, providing requested private features, protecting accounts and systems, preventing misuse, troubleshooting, and responding to enquiries. These interests are assessed against the rights and interests of the people affected.
  • Contract: where processing is necessary to provide a feature that is expressly supplied under a contract, including any paid service or digital content if one is offered.
  • Legal obligation: where information must be kept, disclosed, or otherwise handled to comply with law.
  • Consent: only where a specific activity requires consent, such as non-exempt device storage or another genuinely optional use. Consent can be withdrawn without affecting processing that was lawful before withdrawal.

Storage and access technologies are also subject to the separate UK rules described in the Cookie Notice.

Where information comes from

Information normally comes directly from you, from your browser or device when it connects, from Cloudflare's delivery and security services, or from a connected service that an authorised user deliberately asks a feature to use. Security logs may derive approximate country or risk information from network data.

Public pages can be viewed without an account. Authentication data, essential cookies, challenge results, and basic security records are required to use protected services. AI prompts, uploads, and optional profile information are provided only when the relevant feature is used.

Public pages and external resources

Public pages do not currently use third-party behavioural advertising or cross-site visitor tracking. Hosting and security providers receive ordinary request information such as IP address, user agent, requested URL, and time because that information is needed to deliver and secure the site.

Public bio pages display only profile details selected for publication. The go.mas0ng.com short-link service and social-app handoff use operator-controlled destination formats rather than arbitrary visitor-supplied redirect destinations.

The site loads ABeeZee through Google Fonts, which causes the browser to contact Google and disclose ordinary request information including the browser's IP address. Public pages may also ask the authentication service whether an existing session is valid.

Cookies and browser storage

Public pages may store a small local preference after you dismiss an informational notice. Protected features use essential authentication and security storage, and some tools use local or session storage for requested functionality.

The Cookie Notice identifies the current storage, explains when consent or a legal exception is relied on, and describes available browser controls.

AI, uploads, and Filestore

AI prompts, relevant conversation context, selected settings, and readable attachment content may be sent to OpenAI or DeepSeek to generate a response. Supported images and PDFs may be sent to OpenAI for text extraction even when another provider is selected for the later conversation. See the AI Policy.

Filestore files and metadata are stored on an owner-controlled Media0 filesystem. Access may pass through Cloudflare-hosted Workers for delivery, authentication, authorisation, and security controls. Authorised create, upload, rename, or delete actions may require an additional short-lived server-control session.

The appropriate mechanism depends on the provider, destination, role, and account arrangement in use. You can ask for information about the transfer safeguards that apply to a particular feature by emailing mason@mas0ng.com.

Retention

  • Privacy-notice preferences remain until cleared or replaced by a later version.
  • Normal authentication sessions expire after about seven days; the cross-subdomain bootstrap cookie expires after about 90 seconds. Either can end sooner on logout, revocation, or security invalidation.
  • Login challenges normally expire after five minutes, QR login requests after two minutes, and failed-attempt rate-limit records after 15 minutes.
  • AI chats remain until deleted. Attached files remain for pinned chats and the five most recent unpinned chats by default; older unpinned attachments may be removed while chat text remains.
  • Filestore files remain until an authorised user deletes them or they are removed for security, maintenance, legal, capacity, or service-discontinuation reasons. Deleted material may persist temporarily in backups until those backups rotate.
  • Security reports and contact messages are kept for as long as reasonably necessary to investigate, respond, prevent repeat issues, and maintain an appropriate record.
  • Service providers may keep their own operational or security records under their applicable settings and policies.

Security and automated checks

Reasonable technical and organisational measures are used to restrict access and protect personal data. Sign-in and protected requests may be logged, rate-limited, challenged, allowed, or blocked using session validity, challenge results, request patterns, and other security signals.

These checks protect private areas. mas0ng.com does not use them to make solely automated decisions about ordinary public visitors that have legal or similarly significant effects. No internet service can guarantee absolute security.

Your data protection rights

Depending on the circumstances and lawful basis, UK data protection law may give you rights to be informed; access personal data; correct inaccurate data; request erasure; restrict processing; receive portable data; and challenge certain automated decisions. Rights are not absolute and identity or authority may need to be verified before a request is fulfilled.

Your right to object: Where processing relies on legitimate interests, you may object to that processing. If the law requires the objection to be honoured, the processing will stop unless there are compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or the processing is needed for legal claims.

Send rights requests to mason@mas0ng.com. Requests are normally answered within one month, subject to any lawful extension or exemption.

Data protection complaints

If you believe mas0ng.com has not handled personal data lawfully, email mason@mas0ng.com and make clear that you are raising a data protection complaint. Include enough information to identify the issue, the relevant feature, and what outcome you are seeking. If you complain for someone else, evidence of authority may be required.

The complaint will be acknowledged within 30 days of receipt. Appropriate enquiries will be made without undue delay, you will be kept informed where an investigation takes time, and the outcome will be communicated without undue delay.

If you remain dissatisfied, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office or seek another remedy available under UK law.

Children

mas0ng.com is not directed to children under 13. Protected services for a person under 18 should be enabled only where the operator has specifically authorised access and the feature is appropriate for that person.

If a service is likely to be accessed by children, UK data protection law may require age-appropriate design, risk assessment, high privacy defaults, data minimisation, and other child-specific safeguards even where children are not the intended audience.
